Specific Absorbed Fraction (SAF)

The photon absorbed fraction ϕ is simply the fraction of the emitted photon energy by the source that is absorbed in the region of interest [Brownell et al., 1968; Howell et al., 1999]. The absorbed fraction can only be determined after specifying the source and the geometry of the absorber, the photon energy and the material medium [Brownell et al., 1968].

The absorbed fraction can be expressed as the proportion of the photon energy emitted by the source that is absorbed by the target; this relationship is given by Equation 1 [Brownell et al., 1968].

ϕ = photon energy absorbed by target photon energy emitted by source
(1)

Once the absorbed fraction is obtained, it is possible to determine the specific absorbed fraction (SAF) given the mass of the target; thus, the SAF is the ratio of the absorbed fraction to the mass of the target in kg−1 [Bolch et al., 2009]. This ratio is given by Equation 2.

ϕ(rT ← rS, Ei, t) = ϕ(rT ← rS, Ei, t) M(rT, t)
(2)

Where ϕ(rT ← rS, Ei, t) is the absorbed fraction (defined as the fraction of radiation energy Ei emitted within the source tissue rS at time t that is absorbed in the target tissue rT), and M(rT, t) is the time-dependent mass of the target tissue rT in the reference individual [Bolch et al., 2009].

Calculating SAF values can be challenging due to the complexity of how radiation interacts with the human body; however, this calculation can be performed by taking into account the energy transferred from a source to a target region using computational phantoms that represent the human body, combined with Monte Carlo simulations.

How were SAFs calculated in this study?

The SAF values in this study were calculated based on the deposited energy per unit mass using the t-deposit tally available in PHITS. Since the results are returned in Gy/source, which corresponds to J·kg−1, it is therefore possible to calculate the SAF using Equation 3.

SAF(rT ← rS) = DPHITS(rT ← rS) Ei,rS
(3)

Where DPHITS rT ← rS is the absorbed dose obtained using the PHITS 3.34 Monte Carlo code in J·kg−1, for a source-target organ pair, and Ei,rS is the energy emitted by the source organ in J. With this approach it is possible to calculate SAF values for various scenarios; however, some organs and tissues have more than one segmented region, such as the kidneys (cortex, medulla and pelvis). In these cases, the SAFs were obtained using Equation 4, which represents a weighted average of the SAF values of each subregion of the corresponding organ/tissue.

SAForgan(rT ← rS) = Σi=1n mi SAFi,rT ← rS MOrgan
(4)

Where MOrgan is the total mass of all subregions of the organ or tissue, and mi is only the mass of the subregion, and SAFi rT ← rS are the SAF values obtained for the subregion. This approach was used for all organs/tissues that have subregions, or bilateral organs.
